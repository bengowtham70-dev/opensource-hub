# What open source actually means

When software is "open source", the human-readable recipe (the source code) is public. Anyone can read how it works, check what it does on their computer, and — depending on the license — modify it or share improved versions.

## Why people trust it more

A closed program is a black box. You press a button and hope. An open-source project can be inspected by anyone: security researchers, curious engineers, your own IT department. Problems get found and fixed in public.

## Why it's usually free

Most open-source projects are built by people solving their own problems, companies sharing infrastructure they all need, or communities around a shared tool. The code costs nothing to copy, so it usually costs nothing to use.

## Free ≠ abandoned

The better question is never "is it free?" but "is it healthy?" — is it still getting updates? Do maintainers answer issues? That's exactly what this app's health signals are for.
