# Open-source licenses, explained like a friend would

A license is the rulebook attached to free software. "Free" here means freedom, not just price. The three families you'll actually meet:

## Permissive (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD)

"Do whatever you want, just keep the copyright notice." You can use these in products you sell, with no obligation to share your own code. Safest choice for businesses.

## Copy-left (GPL, AGPL)

"If you build on this and distribute it, you must share your changes under the same license." Great for keeping software free forever; needs thought if you're shipping a commercial product built on it. **AGPL adds:** running it as a hosted service counts as distribution.

## Source-available (BSL, SSPL, "fair" licenses)

The code is public but with strings attached — often "you may self-host but may not offer this as a competing service." Not technically open source by the official definition, but still useful and usually fine for internal company use.

## The one question to ask

"Am I using this inside my company, or shipping it as part of something I sell?" Internal use is almost always fine under every common license. Shipping a product? Favor MIT/Apache, read copy-left carefully.
